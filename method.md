# Method

When investigating a rule system, use Mathematica to simulate the domain directly—chemical reactions, biological processes, physical dynamics, entity relationships—drawing on the Wolfram Knowledgebase and running actual computations against curated data. Use SPIN and UPPAAL to exhaustively explore finite state spaces and timed automata. Use Alloy to find counterexamples in bounded relational models. Use PRISM for probabilistic model checking where uncertainty is intrinsic. Use Z3, CVC5, or Yices to solve constraints and identify satisfying assignments at system boundaries. Use TLA+ to specify and check concurrent and distributed protocols. Use Isabelle, Lean, or ACL2 where their libraries or automation suit the domain better than Coq. Let these tools reveal where the system's behavior diverges from its stated rules, where edge cases cluster, and where practitioners have built elaborate guardrails. Classify what you find: *physical* constraints (from natural law), *institutional* constraints (from convention), and *workaround* constraints (managing an unresolved problem). A simulation of 1840s surgical outcomes would show mortality clustering around duration—not because long surgeries are inherently dangerous, but because consciousness made speed necessary. A simulation of 1840s obstetric wards would show mortality clustering by physician, not patient—contamination, not constitution. Semmelweis and Morton did not optimize these systems; they collapsed the workaround constraints entirely.

Encode what survives in Coq. The proof architecture will reveal which axioms were load-bearing for correctness and which were load-bearing only for coping. Where removing a constraint collapses entire proof obligations into trivialities, you have found a constraint everyone treats as fixed but which is merely unresolved. Simulation finds candidates; model checking finds them through exhaustive state exploration; constraint solving finds them through satisfiability; theorem proving confirms which are eliminable through proof. That is where the next elimination-style breakthrough hides: not in optimizing the coping mechanism, but in removing the need for it.
